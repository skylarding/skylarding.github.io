About

Hi, I'm Skylar. I work in computational psycholinguistics: I use language models as models of linguistic input and of human expectation, and test their predictions against how children learn words and how adults read in a first and a second language.

I am a second-year MA student in Linguistics at UC Davis, advised by Emily Morgan. Two questions run through my current projects. First, how much of the order in which children learn words can be read off the structure of the input alone? With Masoud Jasbi and Zachary Houghton, I relate the semantic geometry of child-scale language models (BabyLM) to age-of-acquisition norms. Second, do second-language readers weight syntactic and semantic cues differently from native readers? With Emily Morgan, I decompose GPT-2 surprisal into syntactic and semantic parts and relate each to eye-movement data from L1 and L2 readers. Earlier, for my BA thesis with Hong Liu, I ran a visual-world eye-tracking study on prediction in Chinese–English bilinguals.

I am applying to PhD programs for Fall 2027. Before Davis, I did my BA in English Studies at Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University. Outside research I paint in watercolor and ink. Email: cyiding [at] ucdavis [dot] edu.

Research

Cue-weighted surprisal in L1 and L2 reading

with Emily Morgan, UC Davis · 2026–present

A test of the Shallow Structure Hypothesis with language model surprisal. I decompose GPT-2 surprisal into a syntactic component (probability mass over words sharing a part of speech) and a semantic component via the chain rule, with word frequency as a separate predictor, and model first-pass reading times from the Multilingual Eye-movement Corpus (45 L1 and 497 L2 readers from 12 language backgrounds) with mixed-effects models, including per-L1-background comparisons.

Age of acquisition in child-scale language models

with Masoud Jasbi and Zachary Houghton, UC Davis · 2026–present

Does the order in which children learn words reflect the semantic structure of what they hear? I treat language models trained on child-scale corpora as models of the input, extract contextual embeddings over CHILDES utterances from 18 checkpoints across three architectures (OPT, GPT-2, RoBERTa) and data scales from 10M to 180B words, and use measures of semantic-neighborhood structure (cohesion, distinctiveness, diversity) to predict Wordbank age-of-acquisition norms alongside the classic predictors. I also built a corpus-based age-of-acquisition measure from children's first productions in CHILDES.

Pragmatic vulnerability in LLM-based content analysis

with Seth Frey, Shiyi Chen, Ziyu Zhao and Miner Ye, Department of Communication, UC Davis · 2026–present

Which variables in a content-analysis codebook are hard for a language model to extract, and why? We draw on common ground theory, Gricean pragmatics and register analysis to classify variables as pragmatically vulnerable or routine, and adapt semantic entropy (sampled extractions clustered by bidirectional entailment) as a variable-level reliability diagnostic over 55 peer-reviewed articles and 57 variables.

Proficiency and cognitive efficiency in L2 predictive sentence processing

with Hong Liu, XJTLU · BA thesis, 2024–2025

A visual-world eye-tracking experiment (Tobii Pro Spectrum, 1200 Hz) with 143 Chinese–English bilinguals, adding a flanker task to measure cognitive processing efficiency. Fixation-based and saccade-based measures of anticipation were analyzed with mixed-effects models. The manuscript is under review at Second Language Research.
