About

Hi, I'm Skylar. I work in computational psycholinguistics: I use language models as models of linguistic input and of human expectation, and test their predictions against how adults read in a first and a second language and how children learn words.

I am a second-year MA student in Linguistics at UC Davis, advised by Emily Morgan. Two questions run through my current projects. First, do second-language readers weight syntactic and semantic cues differently from native readers? With Emily Morgan, I decompose GPT-2 surprisal into syntactic and semantic parts and relate each to eye-movement data from L1 and L2 readers. Second, how much of the order in which children learn words can be read off the structure of the input alone? With Masoud Jasbi and Zachary Houghton, I relate the semantic geometry of child-scale language models (BabyLM) to age-of-acquisition norms. Earlier, for my BA thesis with Hong Liu, I ran a visual-world eye-tracking study on prediction in Chinese–English bilinguals.

I am applying to PhD programs for Fall 2027. Before Davis, I did my BA in English Studies at Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University.
