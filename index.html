Chenyi (Skylar) Ding

MA student in Linguistics, University of California, Davis

I am a second-year MA student in Linguistics at UC Davis, where I work with Emily Morgan on computational psycholinguistics. Most of my work uses language models as a stand-in for the statistics of linguistic experience and asks how far those statistics go in explaining what people do.

With Emily Morgan, I am studying whether second-language readers rely on syntactic and semantic cues in the same way native readers do. Rather than treating language model surprisal as a single black-box predictor, we decompose it into a syntactic and a semantic component and ask whether the two components predict reading times differently for native and non-native readers. With Masoud Jasbi, I am asking how much of the order in which children learn words can be predicted from the semantic structure of child-directed speech, using language models trained on child-scale corpora as a proxy for that input. The same question, what language models capture and what they miss, runs through my work with Seth Frey’s Computational Communication Lab, where we study why large language models used to code academic texts fail on some variables but not others, and use pragmatic theory to predict where those failures will land.

Before Davis, I worked with Hong Liu and Darcy Sperlich at Xi’an Jiaotong-Liverpool University on two eye-tracking projects on second-language processing. With Hong Liu, I designed and ran a visual-world experiment on prediction in Chinese–English bilinguals, from stimulus norming through data collection and analysis; the paper is under review at Second Language Research. As a research assistant to Hong Liu and Darcy Sperlich, I built the reading stimuli and ran a sentence-reading eye-tracking study on how sentence predictability affects lexical processing.

I will be applying to PhD programs for Fall 2027, and plan to keep working at the border between computational modeling and experimental psycholinguistics.
